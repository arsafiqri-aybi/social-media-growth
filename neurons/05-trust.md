# HPC-05 — Trust

## Definition

Trust is the willingness to rely on a source or relationship under uncertainty because the source is perceived as sufficiently credible, honest, competent, reliable, or well-intentioned.

Trust is related to credibility but should not be treated as identical to expertise, attractiveness, or familiarity.

## Underlying mechanisms

In source evaluation, audiences infer qualities such as:

- trustworthiness / integrity
- expertise / competence
- authenticity / genuineness
- goodwill / perceived concern
- consistency and track record

These cues reduce uncertainty about whether information or recommendations are safe to rely on.

## Evidence

A 2024 meta-analysis synthesized 250 effect sizes from 53 studies and identified source credibility theory as the strongest explanatory framework among those tested. Trustworthiness, credibility, and expertise were consistently related to attitudinal and behavioral outcomes, with substantial heterogeneity across contexts.

The same meta-analysis reported possible publication bias in some relationships, so effect magnitudes should not be treated as universal constants.

## Boundary conditions

- Trust is domain-specific: expertise in one topic does not guarantee credibility in another.
- Platform, geography, product type, and influencer type can alter effects.
- Transparency can support trust, while undisclosed persuasion can damage it.
- Familiarity is not equivalent to trust.
- High engagement is not proof of credibility.

## Social-media relevance

Trust helps explain why audiences:
- accept information;
- follow advice;
- return to a source;
- tolerate ambiguity;
- recommend a creator to others.

## Core connections

- **Identity/Homophily -> Trust**: similarity can contribute to trust and favorable attitudes. **Moderately supported.**
- **Connection -> Trust**: parasocial/relational engagement and trust co-occur and can mutually strengthen outcomes, but direction is not universally causal. **Moderately supported.**
- **Trust -> Connection**: reliable interaction can support relational attachment. **Moderately supported.**
- **Trust -> Valuation/Action**: credibility changes how messages and recommendations are evaluated. **Strongly supported.**
- **Trust -> Transmission**: plausible through confidence in source/message, but direct general edge remains **Emerging**.
