# HPC-07 — Social Transmission

## Definition

Social transmission is the movement of information from one person to another through sharing, forwarding, mentioning, recommending, discussing, quoting, or reproducing content.

Virality is one possible aggregate outcome; it is not the psychological mechanism itself.

## Underlying mechanisms

Candidate mechanisms include:

- emotional activation / arousal;
- practical or informational value;
- identity expression and group relevance;
- social norms;
- relationship maintenance;
- self-presentation.

Not all mechanisms currently have equal evidence strength in this repository.

## Evidence

Berger & Milkman's field and experimental work showed that emotional arousal can causally affect transmission and that useful/interesting/surprising content also relates to virality.

Later multi-platform field research also supports roles for emotion and information characteristics, while demonstrating that effects depend on content and context.

Group-identity research shows that social influence is partly contingent on self-categorization and group membership, supporting identity as an important connector into transmission.

## Boundary conditions

- "Emotional" does not automatically mean shareable.
- High-arousal effects are probabilistic, not deterministic.
- Platform affordances and audience norms matter.
- Sharing can be driven by private utility, social identity, coordination, or interpersonal motives.
- Virality metrics confound psychology with distribution systems.

## Social-media relevance

This node explains why content leaves the original viewer and enters another person's attention environment.

## Core connections

- **Emotion -> Social Transmission**: arousal can raise sharing probability. **Strongly supported in bounded content contexts.**
- **Identity -> Social Transmission**: group identity and norms shape influence and expression. **Strongly supported at general social-influence level.**
- **Trust -> Social Transmission**: confidence in source/message may support recommendation, but general causal strength is **Emerging**.
- **Connection -> Social Transmission**: advocacy through relational attachment is plausible but **Emerging**.
- **Transmission -> Attention (new audience)**: mechanically true at system level; psychological response still depends on the receiving person's other nodes.
