# HPC-08 — Reinforcement & Habit

## Definition

This node explains recurrence.

- **Reinforcement / reward learning**: outcomes change the learned value of cues and actions.
- **Habit**: behavior becomes more automatically triggered by recurring contexts after repeated performance.

"Return" is treated as an observable behavior, not as the underlying mechanism.

## Underlying mechanisms

Habit research emphasizes repeated responses in recurring contexts. As repetition builds context-response associations, behavior can become less dependent on active deliberation.

Reward history can also alter attentional priority, creating a bridge from learning back into perception.

## Evidence

Wood & Rünger's Annual Review characterizes habits as repeated responses learned in recurring contexts and describes interaction between habit and deliberate goal pursuit.

Value-driven attention research shows that prior reward learning can make associated cues more likely to capture later attention.

## Boundary conditions

- Repetition alone is not always sufficient for habit.
- Reward is not synonymous with dopamine.
- Habitual behavior and deliberate goals can cooperate or conflict.
- Returning to a creator can result from goals, curiosity, trust, connection, or external reminders without being a habit.

## Social-media relevance

This node can help explain:
- repeated checking;
- recurring consumption routines;
- automatic response to familiar cues;
- stable return patterns;
- learned preference for certain creators/topics.

## Core connections

- **Reinforcement -> Attention**: reward history biases later attentional selection. **Strongly supported.**
- **Valuation <-> Reinforcement**: outcomes update learned value and learned value guides future behavior. **Established at general learning level.**
- **Connection -> Reinforcement/Return**: relational value may support recurrence, but direct habit formation edge is **Emerging**.
- **Curiosity -> Reinforcement/Return**: successful information seeking may motivate repeated exploration, but direct habit causality is **Emerging**.
- **Attention -> Reinforcement**: attention enables exposure/learning, but this is an enabling relation rather than proof of reinforcement.
