# HPC-04 — Identity

## Definition

Identity refers to representations of the self and of group membership that influence interpretation, evaluation, affiliation, and behavior.

For this architecture, identity includes both personal self-concept and social/group identity.

## Underlying mechanisms

Self-categorization and group identification can change:
- who is perceived as "us";
- which norms become relevant;
- which sources carry influence;
- what information feels self-relevant;
- what behavior signals belonging.

Social influence is therefore not only a popularity effect; it can be identity-dependent.

## Evidence

Spears' Annual Review synthesizes evidence that group identity is a central explanation for social influence and must be distinguished from generic conformity, interdependence, and network effects.

Influencer meta-analysis also finds meaningful effects of homophily and consumer-influencer congruence, although effect sizes vary by context and publication-bias concerns exist for some relationships.

## Boundary conditions

- Identity salience changes by context.
- People hold multiple identities that can conflict.
- Similarity/homophily does not always produce trust or behavior.
- Cultural, platform, product, and community context can moderate effects.

## Social-media relevance

Identity helps explain:
- "this is for people like me";
- creator-audience similarity;
- community language and rituals;
- norm adoption;
- loyalty and defense of a community;
- identity-signaling through sharing.

## Core connections

- **Identity -> Social Transmission**: sharing can be shaped by group relevance and social influence. **Strongly supported at group-influence level.**
- **Identity -> Connection**: homophily/similarity can support relational affinity. **Moderately supported.**
- **Identity -> Trust**: perceived similarity can contribute to trust/attitudes, but effects are context-sensitive. **Moderately supported.**
- **Identity -> Valuation**: self-relevance can increase subjective importance. **Moderately supported.**
- **Social norms** act as a connector between Identity and behavior rather than a separate core neuron for now.
