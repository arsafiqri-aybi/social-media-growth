# HPC-03 — Valuation & Emotion

## Definition

This core family contains two distinct but interacting systems:

- **Valuation**: assigning subjective importance, utility, reward, relevance, or expected benefit/cost.
- **Emotion**: affective states that alter experience, action readiness, memory, attention, and social behavior.

They are grouped at core level for architectural economy, not because they are identical.

## Underlying mechanisms

Valuation integrates factors such as expected reward, relevance, information value, goals, and learned associations.

Emotion varies along dimensions including valence and arousal. For social transmission, arousal can matter independently of positive/negative valence.

## Evidence

Reward associations can later bias attention.

Berger & Milkman's observational and experimental work found that high-arousal emotions such as awe, anger, and anxiety were associated with greater online transmission, while low-arousal sadness was associated with less sharing. Their experimental work supported a causal role of activation/arousal in transmission.

## Boundary conditions

- Emotion is not simply "positive vs negative."
- High arousal does not guarantee sharing.
- Value is person- and context-dependent.
- Immediate engagement value can conflict with long-term value or trust.
- Effects found in one content domain should not be assumed universal.

## Social-media relevance

Valuation and emotion help explain:
- whether a post feels personally relevant;
- whether information is worth continued attention;
- whether a message is remembered or acted on;
- whether emotional activation contributes to sharing.

## Core connections

- **Valuation -> Attention**: learned value biases attentional selection. **Strongly supported.**
- **Curiosity <-> Valuation**: information can have subjective value. **Strongly supported.**
- **Emotion -> Social Transmission**: arousal can increase sharing probability. **Strongly supported in bounded contexts.**
- **Identity -> Valuation**: identity-relevant information can acquire greater subjective significance. **Moderately supported at theory/review level.**
- **Reinforcement -> Valuation**: experienced outcomes update learned value. **Established at general learning level.**
