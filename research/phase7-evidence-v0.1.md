# Phase 7 Evidence Registry v0.1

Date: 2026-10-05

## Audience / value

### P7-E01 — Ju, Chocarro & Martín Martín
Type: systematic review  
Title: *Value creation in mobile social media: a systematic review and agenda for future research*  
Journal: Baltic Journal of Management  
DOI: 10.1108/BJM-04-2021-0157  
Coverage: 53 articles  
URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/org/science/article/pii/S174652652100039X

Use:
Supports multidimensional, actor/context-dependent value creation and co-creation.

### P7-E02 — Salminen et al. (2023)
Type: systematic review  
Title: *How can algorithms help in segmenting users and customers? A systematic review and research agenda for algorithmic customer segmentation*  
Journal: Journal of Marketing Analytics  
DOI: 10.1057/s41270-023-00235-5  
Coverage: 172 articles  
URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41270-023-00235-5

Use:
Supports segmentation as an analytical activity while showing substantial methodological heterogeneity and evaluation limitations. Domain validity cannot be inferred from clustering alone.

### P7-E03 — Le (2022)
Type: systematic review  
Title: *Customer brand co-creation on social media: a systematic review*  
DOI: 10.1108/MIP-04-2022-0161  
Coverage: 59 articles  
URL: https://www.emerald.com/mip/article-abstract/40/8/1038/298757

Use:
Supports value co-creation as antecedent/consequence network rather than a single metric.

### P7-E04 — Geng et al. (2021)
Type: empirical two-study research  
Title: *The effects of ad social and personal relevance on consumer ad engagement on social media: The moderating role of platform trust*  
URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0747563221001576

Use:
Supports relevance as a bounded candidate pathway to engagement. Advertising scope limits transport to all organic creator content.

## Communication / response

### P7-E05 — YouTube Help
Type: official platform documentation  
Title: *Understand your content performance for YouTube’s recommendation system*  
Retrieved: 2026-10-05  
URL: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/16559650?hl=en

Use:
Operationally separates Appeal, Engagement and Satisfaction. Useful platform evidence, not a universal psychological taxonomy.

### P7-E06 — Schreiner, Fischer & Riedl
Type: literature review  
Title: *Impact of content characteristics and emotion on behavioral engagement in social media: literature review and research agenda*  
Journal: Electronic Commerce Research  
DOI: 10.1007/s10660-019-09353-8  
Coverage: 45 studies

Use:
Shows heterogeneous, often inconclusive content-characteristic effects and highlights sender/receiver/medium confounds and limited mediation evidence.

## Relationship / retention

### P7-E11 — Han & Balabanis (2024)
Type: meta-analysis  
Title: *Meta-analysis of social media influencer impact: Key antecedents and theoretical foundations*  
Journal: Psychology & Marketing  
DOI: 10.1002/mar.21927  
Coverage: 53 studies; 250 effect sizes

Use:
Supports distinct credibility, trustworthiness, expertise, congruity, homophily and parasocial mechanisms; effect heterogeneity across contexts.

### P7-E12 — Aw & Fernando (2026)
Type: systematic review  
Title: *Consumers’ parasocial interactions and relationships with online influencers: a systematic review*  
Journal: Journal of Consumer Marketing  
DOI: 10.1108/JCM-02-2024-6611  
Coverage: 76 peer-reviewed articles  
URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S073637612600025X

Use:
Supports PSI/PSR as distinct constructs with source, message, medium and consumer antecedents plus multiple outcomes/moderators.

## Measurement / learning

### P7-E13 — Ascani & Ancillai (2026)
Type: systematic literature review  
Title: *Social media marketing and performance measurement: does it take two to tango?*  
Journal: Review of Managerial Science  
DOI: 10.1007/s11846-025-00891-0  
URL: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11846-025-00891-0

Use:
Supports integrated performance-measurement process and identifies fragmentation/challenges in narrow KPI-centric approaches.

### P7-E14 — Boegershausen et al. (2025)
Type: review of platform studies / methodology guidance  
Title: *On the persistent mischaracterization of Google and Facebook A/B tests: How to conduct and report online platform studies*  
Journal: International Journal of Research in Marketing  
DOI: 10.1016/j.ijresmar.2024.12.004  
Coverage: 133 published online platform studies  
URL: https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167811624001149

Use:
Critical guardrail: platform A/B tools can lack true random assignment of ads to consumers because targeting/delivery algorithms confound condition exposure. Do not label such results causal by default.

## Evidence note

These sources justify Phase 7 decomposition and guardrails. They do not establish universal effect magnitudes or runtime coefficients.
