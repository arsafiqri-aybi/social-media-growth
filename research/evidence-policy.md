# Evidence Policy

## Objective

Prevent the neural architecture from becoming a collection of plausible-sounding psychology claims.

## Source hierarchy

Prefer, in order:

1. meta-analysis
2. systematic review
3. replicated / preregistered experiments
4. strong experimental studies
5. longitudinal / behavioral studies
6. strong observational studies
7. theoretical reviews for mechanism integration
8. domain-specific surveys for context, not universal causal claims

## Claim protocol

For every material claim:

1. write it atomically;
2. identify whether it is descriptive, correlational, causal, mechanistic, or inferential;
3. find evidence matching that exact claim;
4. record population and context;
5. record contradictory or limiting evidence;
6. assign confidence;
7. avoid generalizing beyond the evidence.

## Causality rule

- Correlation does not become causation because it is statistically significant.
- Meta-analysis of correlational studies still does not automatically establish causality.
- Experimental evidence can support causality only within the manipulated construct and tested context.

## Confidence labels

- Established
- Strongly supported
- Moderately supported
- Emerging
- Hypothesis

## Failure awareness

Reject or flag:
- "dopamine hack" language without precise mechanism;
- universal attention-span claims without valid evidence;
- deterministic virality claims;
- claims that one emotion always increases sharing;
- engagement used as proof of trust;
- familiarity used as proof of credibility;
- platform metrics treated as pure measures of psychology.

## Update policy

The architecture is versioned. New evidence may:
- strengthen an edge;
- weaken an edge;
- split a node;
- merge overlapping nodes;
- add a connector mechanism;
- retire unsupported claims.

Do not expand architecture merely because more concepts exist. Expansion must materially improve explanatory, retrieval, predictive, or operational value.
